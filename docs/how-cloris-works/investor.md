# How Cloris Works

*Architecture brief for investors. Six chapters, ~30 minutes. Each chapter walks one of the structural commitments behind the company's defensibility thesis.*

---

## Chapter 1 — The Brief

The recruiting-tools category has a durability problem. Every meaningful data source — OpenAlex, USPTO, PubMed, GitHub, most of LinkedIn — is open or commoditizing. Tools that compete on data acquisition are competing on a vanishing surface. The only durable asset in the category is the one almost no incumbent has built: a calibrated way to evaluate the data against a specific role. The first place Cloris makes that bet visible is in the brief.

A brief in Cloris is a structured object, not a search query. It is defined by the V2 schema (`shared/brief_schema.py:179-260`) and built to carry the parts of role judgment that recruiters actually use and most data models don't represent: capability areas, depth distinction, non-fit patterns, employer signal rules, calibration examples, and facial-calibration thresholds. The field set is bifurcated by mutability. Locked fields — role title, geography, project ID, market density — pin what the role *is*. Mutable fields — instructions, search priorities, calibration examples — are what the closed feedback loop is allowed to revise. The split is in `shared/brief_iteration.py:36-63`, and it exists because a feedback-driven revision must be able to sharpen a role without accidentally redefining it.

Three structural choices in the brief carry the company's defensibility thesis. None looks especially clever in isolation. Together they are the architectural commitment that makes everything else possible.

The first is that briefs are versioned, and the system fails closed. When a downstream stage needs "the canonical brief for this run," resolution requires a unique highest-version sibling; if any sibling lacks a parseable version suffix, or two tie at the top, the operator must specify explicitly (`shared/recruiter_brief_resolution.py:74-92`). This is a commitment to traceability, and it is the precondition for the closed feedback loop to work at all. A loop that revises the brief based on candidate outcomes can only function if every saved candidate is tied to a known brief version. Tools whose role definition is implicit, prose, or unversioned cannot retrofit this.

The second is that revisions are clamped. The LLM proposes how the brief should change after a run; calibration thresholds can move by at most 0.10 per revision (`shared/brief_iteration.py:71`). This is how the substrate prevents catastrophic forgetting. Weeks of converged calibration cannot be erased by one bad run. The closed loop is monotone in expectation, not stochastic — which is also why it can be trusted to run unattended over a quarter without operator vigilance, the operational property the product is sold against.

The third is that the brief is the substrate for evaluation, not retrieval. The same object that pins what a role is also feeds the four-step judgment template — capability mapping → depth test → transferability → decision — defined in `shared/judger.py` and `linkedin/judgment_templates.py`. This is the architectural seam where Cloris diverges from every general-purpose sourcing tool. Per the product north star §6: data is commoditized; calibrated evaluation is not. Calibration compounds with feedback, and the brief is the only place that compounding accumulates.

The strong claim defended here is that a generalist sourcing tool cannot graft a closed feedback loop onto its product later. The loop requires the brief to be a structured, versioned, calibration-bearing object from the beginning, and to be the canonical source that every other layer reads from. Tools that store role definitions as prose, or split them across separate query and judgment configurations, cannot retroactively unify them without rebuilding the data model. The brief is the architectural commitment that makes the moat possible. Everything else in Cloris is downstream of this choice.

The financial-story implication is straightforward. Brief authoring is the part of customer onboarding that is intentionally founder-led and high-touch (north star §7). It is also the part that compounds. Every customer's brief library, refined over months of feedback, is a switching-cost surface that grows with use. The longest-pole product investment — the candidate workspace, the feedback artifact table, run-to-brief pinning (north star §9) — is also the largest source of long-term commercial defensibility. The two are not separate questions. They are the same question.

## Chapter 2 — The Search

The conventional category architecture treats sources as adapters of one search abstraction: rank-by-relevance over a multi-source corpus, with per-source connectors hidden behind a common interface. That model fails on closer inspection. LinkedIn requires browser automation, anti-detection discipline, humanized timing, decoy interactions, and a search-intelligence layer that tracks drift between expected and observed results — the entire `linkedin/search_intelligence.py` apparatus exists because Recruiter sessions are detection-sensitive and rate-policed. GitHub is API-only, with no detection risk, social-graph traversal, contribution-pattern signals, and a completely different governance model (`github/orchestrator.py` doc comment: *"Pure API-based. Mirrors orchestrator.py's Pipeline pattern"*). Researcher discovery (OpenAlex/PubMed/arXiv, ORCID, Semantic Scholar) is again different — citation-linked, identity-rich, with lineage signals that LinkedIn does not carry. Treating them as one abstraction either flattens these differences (losing per-source signal) or smuggles them into the orchestrator (losing modularity).

Cloris's architecture refuses the false equivalence. Each source has its own orchestrator (`linkedin/orchestrator.py`, `github/orchestrator.py`), and each is allowed to be different where it must be. What is shared is a substrate — a common candidate lifecycle, a common evaluation procedure, a common runtime store, a common closed loop — into which any new orchestrator slots.

The shared substrate's discriminator is `source` as a column on every relevant table (`shared/runtime_state/store.py:121-205`): `runs.source`, `work_units.source`, `candidates.source`. The candidates table enforces `UNIQUE(brief_id, source, identity_key)` (`shared/runtime_state/store.py:182`) — the same person discovered on two sources is two rows under the discrimination contract. Cross-source identity resolution is a separate layer (`shared/cross_module_identity/`, scoped in the architecture north star §10), with adapter functions per `(source_a, source_b)` pair.

This is the architectural choice that lets the company's product roadmap be more than a single-source SaaS. New modules — Researcher (frontier-AI hiring), OSS Maintainers (infrastructure engineer hiring), Designer (creative-technical hiring), Defense (security-cleared hiring), Healthcare — slot into the substrate without renegotiation. The work of adding a module is one new orchestrator following a stable shape, one new judgment template, one new identity adapter. Per the architecture north star §8, the integration contract is documented and enforced. The substrate absorbs the new module rather than having to be rebuilt for it.

The bounded-search property matters for a different reason. Both pipelines plan a finite set of queries before issuing any of them, execute them, and stop with one of a small set of recorded reasons: `score_above_threshold`, `high_confidence_match`, `no_new_urls`, `single_surface_name_variant_stop`, `plan_exhausted` (`shared/recruiter_identity_schemas.py:170-178`). The implication is that recall is auditable. A run can be inspected after the fact: here is what we planned, here is what we tried, here is why each search ended. This is one of the load-bearing operational properties the product is sold against. A skeptical recruiter or a pilot-program operator can interrogate the run rather than taking the result on faith. Generalist sourcing tools cannot offer this because their search is stateless and unplanned.

Two architectural corollaries worth naming for the moat thesis. First: cross-source identity as a separate layer, rather than as a constraint at the storage tier, means person-level views are an add-on, not a foundation, and a tool that conflates sources at storage cannot retrofit person-level reconciliation without a data-layer rewrite. Second: the source-namespaced work-unit kinds (`LINKEDIN_STRING_KIND`, `GITHUB_QUERY_KIND`, future `RESEARCHER_AUTHOR_QUERY_KIND`, `MAINTAINER_PACKAGE_QUERY_KIND`, etc., per architecture §2) mean modules can run concurrently, on different state directories, without cross-locking. Multi-module operation in v1 is multiple workers in multiple state directories — a clean operational model that is well-defined today, not a v2 hand-wave.

## Chapter 3 — The Judgment

The strategic asset Cloris has built is the calibrated evaluation pipeline. The product north star §6 is unambiguous about this: data acquisition is commoditized, evaluation is not, and brief calibration is the work the customer cannot easily replicate. This chapter walks the architecture under that claim.

Every candidate, on every source, runs through a four-step structural procedure: capability mapping → depth test → transferability test → decision (`shared/judger.py`, instantiated per-source by `linkedin/judgment_templates.py`, `github/judgment_templates.py`). The procedure is paradigm-neutral by design. It carries no domain vocabulary; the brief supplies the vocabulary at evaluation time. Per the architecture north star §4: *"the procedure is in the brief, not the code… code does not embed role-specific vocabulary; it composes brief-supplied vocabulary into the procedure."* This is why the same evaluation substrate works across ML researchers, defense engineers, biotech computational scientists, and creative-technical roles — without structural change.

The two-stage execution (`facial` triage on surface evidence, `full` deep evaluation on opened evidence) reflects a cost-quality tradeoff that matters for unit economics. Facial decisions are cheap; full decisions are expensive. The system gates full evaluation behind a positive facial. Output vocabulary at each stage is enumerated: `_VALID_FACIAL = {"FACIAL_YES", "FACIAL_NO", "FACIAL_BORDERLINE"}` (`shared/judger.py:38`); full evaluation produces `SAVE`, `REJECT`, or one of three nuanced save modes — `INFERENTIAL_SAVE`, `TRANSFERABLE_SAVE`, `SIGNAL_SAVE` (`shared/bias_controls.py:53-58`). The nuanced modes are how the system records *why* a save is a save, with rationale captured per-candidate. This is upstream of the candidate-workspace product surface; downstream consumers can filter and act on rationale, not just on a binary outcome.

The architectural feature that earns the right to run the pipeline unattended is the bias monitor (`shared/bias_controls.py`). Every decision the system makes is recorded into a per-string and per-session log; after each search string and each page of results, the monitor runs a set of structured checks: `CONSECUTIVE_SAVES`, `CONSECUTIVE_REJECTS`, `SAVE_RATE_SPIKE`, `PARSE_FAILURE_RATE`, `FACIAL_RATE_ANOMALY`, `VOLUME_INVERSION` (`shared/bias_controls.py:44-50`). When a check fires, the monitor produces an Alert with severity. Some alerts are `flag` (log and continue). The most serious are `pause` (stop the current string, surface to the operator). The thresholds and bands come from the brief's `bias_controls` and `facial_calibration` sections (`shared/bias_controls.py:119-130`); a different role gets different thresholds without a code change.

Worth quoting the code comment that names what `CONSECUTIVE_SAVES` defends against: *"Volume-threshold inversion — the Session 5 failure mode where the agent saves everyone because the population 'looks' relevant as a group"* (`shared/bias_controls.py:175-180`). This is the specific kind of compounding error that breaks autonomous-evaluation systems if you do not architect against it from the start. The defense is built in, brief-calibrated, and load-bearing for the operational claim that the system can run unattended over weeks.

The defensibility argument here, stated directly: data acquisition is the part of the recruiting-tools category that VCs love because it is legible, but it is also the part that is commoditizing fastest. Calibrated evaluation, with bias controls that scale with the brief, is the part that compounds with use and is hard to replicate from the outside. A competitor with the same data sources and the same LLM access cannot reproduce a customer's calibrated evaluation pipeline without months of feedback runs against a structured brief that competitors do not have the architecture to store. The moat is not the data, and not the model. It is the substrate that turns recruiter feedback into evaluation quality.

## Chapter 4 — The Run

The runtime substrate is the most under-discussed and most operationally consequential layer of the product. It is also the layer that most distinguishes Cloris from anything else in the category.

Every run writes its canonical state to a SQLite database at `runtime_state.sqlite3`, one per state directory (`shared/runtime_state/store.py`). The schema covers `runs`, `work_units`, `candidates`, `candidate_attempts`, `events`, `side_effects`, with foreign-key cascades and an enforced lifecycle transition table (`shared/runtime_state/store.py:62-71`). The architectural commitments under the substrate are listed in the architecture north star §1; the load-bearing ones for the investor thesis are these.

**Canonical-first.** Stage JSONLs (`snippets.jsonl`, `facial_judgments.jsonl`, `final_judgments.jsonl`), `progress.json`, candidate history files, and search-memory files are projections, not control state (`shared/runtime_state/projections.py`, `Sourcing-Agent-2nd-Gen-Roadmap.md:381-393`). Code that needs to make a runtime decision is forbidden from reading them. Projections drift over time as code evolves; canonical state does not. Bug surfaces are bounded.

**One canonical writer per state directory.** The detached worker process is the only authorized writer for an active state directory (`docs/cloris-ui-spec.md:62-63`). The Cloris UI/API process opens the SQLite file read-only via `sqlite3.connect(f"file:{path}?mode=ro", uri=True)` (`cloris/control_plane.py:8-17`). Two writers in the same state directory is an invariant violation, not a configuration option, enforced by `RuntimeStateLock`.

**Schema migration is gated and idempotent.** Migrations live in `shared/runtime_state/store.py:_migrate` and are gated on `meta.schema_version`. The schema has evolved seven times since project start; legacy data survives every migration. This is the discipline that lets the platform evolve without losing customer state.

**Brief identity is pinned at run-start.** `runs.brief_path_at_launch`, `runs.brief_content_hash`, `runs.brief_snapshot_json` (`shared/runtime_state/store.py:303-317`) capture the brief as it was when the run executed. This is the precondition for the closed feedback loop: feedback can only revise the brief if every saved candidate is tied to a known brief version with its full calibration vocabulary preserved at decision time.

**Lifecycle transitions are enforced at the store layer.** `ALLOWED_LIFECYCLE_TRANSITIONS` (`shared/runtime_state/store.py:62-71`) defines the candidate state graph: `discovered → snippet_extracted → facial_started → facial_terminal → full_started → full_terminal`, with `failed_retryable` and `failed_terminal` as recoverable and absorbing states. Illegal transitions are rejected at write time. New modules cannot invent transitions; they map onto the existing graph.

The implication that compounds over a customer relationship: stop, resume, and crash recovery are properties of the architecture, not features bolted on. A run can be paused mid-search and resumed on a different day from a different process, and the state machine correctly picks up. A worker crash followed by a UI reopen rediscovers the active run via the `worker.json` sidecar (`cloris/worker.py:70-117`). Schema-evolved data from earlier runs continues to inspect cleanly. None of this is the part of the product that closes a sale, but all of it is the part that makes the product not embarrass itself in week three of customer use — and the part that explicitly differentiates it from category competitors built on stateless or ad-hoc-state architectures.

The architectural pattern (event log + projections + enforced lifecycle + canonical-writer) is borrowed from financial systems and durable workflow engines, not from CRUD-shaped recruiting software. It is non-trivial to build retroactively. A competitor building on a CRUD model cannot evolve into this without a data-tier rewrite — which is why this is the layer that, in operational maturity, most clearly separates Cloris from anything else in the category.

## Chapter 5 — The Learning

The closed feedback loop is the long-term moat and the longest-pole product investment, per product north star §9. It is also the layer that turns the substrate's traceability commitments into commercial defensibility.

Two compounding artifacts are produced by every run.

The first is the iterated brief. After every run, brief iteration (`shared/brief_iteration.py`) processes the run's outcomes and the recruiter's feedback into a proposed revision. The proposal mutates only the mutable fields (`MUTABLE_FIELDS`, `shared/brief_iteration.py:37-51`); locked fields — role title, geography, project ID, market density — are immune to revision. Calibration thresholds can move by at most 0.10 (`CALIBRATION_MAX_DELTA = 0.10`, `shared/brief_iteration.py:71`), preventing catastrophic forgetting. The output is a new brief version with a tracked rationale; the recruiter accepts or edits, and the next run launches against the revised brief. Every customer's brief library is therefore a calibrated artifact whose value compounds with use and whose structure cannot be replicated without months of feedback against the substrate.

The second is the per-market intelligence artifact. Every run writes structured evidence to a per-market artifact at `output/market_intelligence/<market_key>/market-intel.json` (`market_intelligence/engine.py:191-200`). The market key is a hash of role title, geography, and role level (`market_intelligence/engine.py:181-188`); multiple briefs in the same market share the artifact. The artifact accumulates: market hypotheses, talent-pool patterns, sourcing implications, employer signal evidence, research opportunities. The synthesis runs through a multi-stage agent pipeline — planner, critic, internal-synthesis backend (`market_intelligence/agent_backends.py`) — so the artifact is interpreted, not just aggregated. The market layer is what powers "Learn about the market," one of the five locked verbs on the home screen — sitting at the same operational level as "Start a search."

The combined loop:

1. The brief is authored, or iterated from a previous version.
2. The run executes against the platforms the brief targets; the runtime state captures every decision and rationale.
3. Recruiters review saves and rejects, mark them, add feedback.
4. Brief iteration proposes a revision based on the feedback and the run's outcome.
5. Market intelligence ingests the run's evidence into the per-market artifact.
6. The next brief revision sees both the recruiter's feedback *and* the updated market context.
7. The next run uses the revised brief, against an updated picture of the market.

This loop is the actual product. The infrastructure underneath — the bounded search, the structural evaluation, the runtime substrate — exists to make the loop trustable and to ensure every artifact the loop produces is tied to a known and recoverable state.

The architectural claim, again, that distinguishes this from generalist sourcing tools: the loop requires every layer to be loop-compatible from the start. A versioned, structured brief; an evaluation procedure that consumes the brief and produces interpretable rationale; a runtime substrate that pins the brief at run start and stores every decision durably; a market layer that aggregates across briefs in the same market; and a UI that surfaces the loop as a primary action rather than burying it in a settings menu. Removing any of these layers breaks the loop. Building any of them retroactively, into a tool that already has customers, requires a data-tier rewrite.

The financial implication: the customer's brief library plus the per-market artifacts is the switching-cost surface. It does not exist on day one of a customer's onboarding; it exists on month three, after enough runs have closed the loop enough times to encode the customer's taste and the customer's market view. Per north star §6: *brief calibration is the work the customer cannot easily replicate*. The corollary is that the customer cannot easily leave, either, without leaving that calibration behind. The longer the customer uses Cloris, the more expensive the alternative becomes — not because of contractual lock-in, but because of asset compounding.

## Chapter 6 — The Surface

A short closing chapter on the part of the product that earns retention rather than acquisition.

Five canonical UI surfaces are defined (`docs/cloris-ui-spec.md:163-251`, architecture north star §7): the Authoring Loop (brief authoring), Launch Gate (per-module readiness checks), Ambient Home (calm landing surface, recent runs, current run summary), Run Review (who Cloris surfaced, why, what happened — person-first when cross-module identity resolution lands), and Next Run Learning (the closed-loop surface that turns recruiter feedback into the next brief revision). The five surfaces are not optional decoration; they are the surface contract for every module Cloris adds. Each surface reads from the same canonical substrate. None of them have private state.

The editorial discipline is binding. The 18 editorial design rules at `docs/cloris-surface-design-rules.md` ship-block any new surface; the PR checklist enumerates per-rule per-surface validation. Voice commitments are captured in product north star §8: editorial, not dashboard; sparse voice that lives at transitions, not inside focused work; high-stakes states drop the voice entirely; Cloris is a person, not a brand mascot.

The investor-relevant claim about the surface is narrow and worth being precise about. The surface does not constitute the moat; the substrate, the brief, the closed loop, and the runtime architecture are the moat. The surface is the layer that earns the recruiter's affection over the months of use required for the loop to compound. Recruiting tools sold today are uniformly unpleasant to look at and to operate — even the ones that work. A premium specialist tool, priced as documented in north star §7 ($1.5K-$4K/seat-month for in-house teams; $50K-$200K+/seat-year for enterprise), has to feel like a tool the recruiter wants to spend two hours a day inside of. The editorial discipline is what makes that retention real rather than aspirational.

That is the architecture, end to end. A structured, versioned brief that holds the calibrated judgment of the role. A bounded search across source-discriminated orchestrators that absorb new modules without rebuilding the substrate. A four-step evaluation procedure with brief-calibrated bias controls that earns the right to run unattended. A SQLite-backed runtime substrate borrowed from durable-workflow architecture, with one-writer discipline and pinned brief identity. A closed feedback loop whose outputs — iterated briefs and per-market artifacts — are the customer's switching-cost surface. An editorial surface that earns the retention required for the loop to compound. Six chapters; one defensibility argument: the calibrated evaluation pipeline is the only durable strategic asset in the recruiting-tools category, and Cloris is the architecture that makes calibrated evaluation possible at all.
